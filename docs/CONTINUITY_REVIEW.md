# Reviewing the continuity comparison

Open the [local gallery](../runs/continuity-round-2026-10-04/review/index.html) after export. It presents four anonymous workflows, each through four successive requests, with six fixed views per stage and a shared starting environment. Generated artifacts are local and excluded from Git.

Start with the overview, garden and forecourt views. Follow one entry through all four stages before comparing it with another. Then use the hero, reverse and detail views to check whether additions still belong to the original environment. Click an image to inspect its full size.

The requested changes are intentional:

1. Extend the environment with connected outdoor areas and furniture.
2. Develop the garden and its working props.
3. Warm the shared stone, widen the table and stool seats, preserve the artist's stool placement, and add wayfinding.
4. Retain exactly one delivered plaque, add lanterns, and widen the sign while preserving earlier work.

Look for continuity in scale, material treatment, construction details, lighting and visual hierarchy. Note which stage first introduces a problem, whether a later request makes it worse, and whether correcting it would require local cleanup or substantial rebuilding. A larger object count does not itself mean a better environment.

Score only what the images support. A score of 4 means usable with minor cleanup; missing or unassessed criteria should stay blank. The requested change descriptions are not completion claims. Native checks, interrupted-operation handling and editability are separate evidence.

Download the scores before reloading the page; entries are not saved automatically. The supplied `scores.json` can also be edited directly. Browser interaction has not been qualified: source, file and pixel checks do not establish that the download button works in your browser.

Keep private assignments and automated critic notes closed until your visual assessment is saved. Afterward, native inspection can assess organization, linked assets, materials, dependencies and ease of making another change. Record actual repair minutes separately from visual scores; neither rendered images nor the automated scene checks establish that effort.
