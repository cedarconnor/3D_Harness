# Courtyard corner

Create a detailed, welcoming Mediterranean courtyard corner in Blender: pale sandstone architecture, warm plaster, aged wood, restrained blue-green paint, bronze details and planted terracotta. Favor legible architectural proportions, a clear focal hierarchy, believable construction and coherent material scale. The scene should look designed, not like a collection of unrelated primitives. No lettering, watermarks or method identifiers in the image.

Build within an approximately 10 m wide by 8 m deep footprint centered at the origin, with the rear wall near Y=3 and floor at Z=0. Keep the front open to the cameras. Include a newly modeled architectural focal element with an arched opening and a newly modeled bench prop near (-1.2, 1.2, 0). Use six to eight meaningful mesh asset families, including repeated placements sharing mesh data. Use at least three shared material families. Add secondary detail only after the larger forms read well. No imported geometry or online asset downloads; supplied textures/HDRI and your own native modeling are the complete kit.

The starter contains only three fixed cameras, a sun, an HDRI world, and a retained shared stone material. There is no example geometry. Preserve all starter cameras, their transforms and optics, the sun, world, resolution, exposure and color management. Additional motivated practical lights are allowed during construction. Use the fixed cameras to assess appearance; camera.hero is the final active camera. Do not add cameras. Cycles, 32 samples, denoising, 960x540, 100%, PNG, AgX Medium High Contrast are fixed. GPU device choice is a runtime setting, not a creative change.

The architectural element and at least two separate scene objects must use the starter material with dcc_material_id='stone'. Its Principled BSDF Base Color is initially (0.55, 0.47, 0.36, 1); keep this input unlinked. You may add surface bump/roughness from the supplied maps at plausible physical scale, but do not replace the material, shader or its base color. Avoid over-contrasted texture detail. Other materials are your choice within the palette.

The bench's principal seat is a single mesh object, instance ID 'hero.seat', asset ID 'bench'. Its evaluated world-space dimensions must be (1.9, 0.50, 0.12) meters, tolerance 0.005 m. Its long axis is world X; top is near Z=0.55. Make the complete bench attractive and structurally plausible; passing the seat dimensions alone is not sufficient. Other bench parts use asset ID 'bench' and their own unique instance IDs. Do not bevel beyond the requested evaluated bounds.

Every scene object has a unique string dcc_instance_id. Each mesh has a meaningful dcc_asset_id shared across its family. Each used material has a unique dcc_material_id. These are common handoff requirements. Do not fabricate one asset family per part to reach the family count. Keep all modeled geometry as ordinary meshes, without Geometry Nodes instancers, linked external libraries, hidden auxiliary geometry or shader groups. Repeated mesh data is supported. No animation or simulations are needed.

Deliver output/pre.blend, packing all used external image dependencies, with camera.hero active. Keep the starter intact. Also deliver output/CONTINUE.md: scene intent, native checkpoint, asset/material identities, dependencies, unfinished work, any tool errors or uncertain mutations, elapsed/render times and capture count. This is the only written handoff a later agent will receive. Scripts/transcript are not included. Do not anticipate a revision that is not in this brief.

Resource ceilings: 75 minutes for construction, with a later 15-minute revision reservation; 30 minutes rendering and at most 24 still captures across both stages. Use 18 or fewer during construction so at least six remain for revision. Two distinct repairs maximum per failed requirement. Record actual usage where available; model cost/tokens may be unavailable. No paid generation or external services. The output is subject to independent technical checks and artist review; do not grade or declare acceptance yourself.
