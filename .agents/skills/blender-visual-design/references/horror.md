# Horror and unsettling environments

Define the intended tension with reference frames and a scene beat: what the
viewer first recognizes, what feels wrong, and what is revealed later. Psychological,
liminal, body-horror and analog approaches use different devices; a dark industrial
corridor is one option rather than a universal horror recipe.

Plan sightlines, thresholds, partial occlusion and one or two narrative clusters.
Use environmental cause and effect: a displaced chair, wear leading to a locked
door, or an abandoned task. Leave quiet space where it helps anticipation instead
of scattering identical grime and clutter everywhere.

Motivate practical lights, select a contrast range that preserves intended cues,
and test the actual display/output transform. Fog, flicker, narrow passages and
colour casts are optional choices. Preserve the project's required door clearances,
navigation and accessibility even when staging claustrophobia. Check any flicker
against the delivery requirements before using it.

For game spaces, visible darkness is not proof of gameplay concealment, and sound
suggested by the set is not an implemented audio system. Check mechanics in the
destination engine when they are part of the brief.

Review the reveal sequence or camera path, the legibility of important objects,
and whether the intended focal cue wins without lifting every shadow. Record
specific successful/failed frames and artist preference. Numeric contrast or an
image-similarity score cannot certify the intended emotional response.
